THEORETICAL BACKGROUND

Understanding independence

Independence describes whether knowing the value of one variable provides information about the other.

What does independence mean?

In probability theory, two events A and B are independent when the occurrence of one does not change the probability of the other. Mathematically, this means that the joint probability equals the product of the marginal probabilities.

Probability definition P(A ∩ B) = P(A) · P(B)

For two categorical random variables X and F, independence means that every combination of their possible values satisfies the following relationship:

Independence of two variables P(X = xᵢ, F = fⱼ) = P(X = xᵢ) · P(F = fⱼ)

An equivalent interpretation is that the conditional distribution of F given X should be the same as the marginal distribution of F. In other words, knowing X should not change the probabilities associated with F.

From observations to a contingency table

A bivariate dataset consists of observations in pairs. Each observation contains one value of X and one value of F. Instead of analyzing every pair individually, we count how often each combination occurs.

These counts form a contingency table. Its rows represent the categories of X, while its columns represent the categories of F. The row and column totals are called marginal frequencies because they summarize one variable without distinguishing the categories of the other.

Expected frequency under independence Eᵢⱼ = (Row totalᵢ × Column totalⱼ) / N

If the variables are independent, the observed counts should be reasonably close to these expected counts. Differences are inevitable in real samples, even when the variables are independent, because of random variation.

The Chi-square statistic

The Pearson Chi-square statistic summarizes the differences between observed and expected frequencies. Each squared difference is divided by its expected frequency, so that deviations are evaluated relative to the size of the expected count.

Pearson Chi-square statistic χ² = Σᵢ Σⱼ (Oᵢⱼ − Eᵢⱼ)² / Eᵢⱼ

The null hypothesis states that X and F are independent. The alternative hypothesis states that they are associated. A large Chi-square statistic indicates that the observed table differs substantially from what independence would predict.

For a table with r rows and c columns, the degrees of freedom are:

Degrees of freedom df = (r − 1)(c − 1)

The usual Chi-square approximation assumes independent observations and sufficiently large expected frequencies. If several expected counts are very small, the approximation may be unreliable and the analysis should be treated with caution.

INTERPRETATION

Association is not causation

A statistical relationship can be informative, but it does not automatically explain why the variables are related.

≠

Association

Statistical association means that the distribution of one variable differs depending on the category of the other. The Chi-square test can provide evidence against the hypothesis of independence.

→

Causality

Causality means that changing one variable produces a change in another. A contingency table alone cannot establish this relationship. Other factors, such as age, experience or external conditions, may influence both variables.

Final considerations

The contingency table provides a clear description of how two categorical variables are distributed together. Marginal and conditional frequencies help reveal patterns, while expected frequencies provide the benchmark required by the Chi-square test.

If the test statistic exceeds the critical value, the null hypothesis of independence is rejected at the selected significance level. This indicates evidence of an association in the population, under the assumptions of the test. If it does not exceed the critical value, there is not enough evidence to reject independence. This does not prove that the variables are independent.

Finally, statistical significance does not measure the practical importance of a relationship and does not establish causality. The context of the data and the way observations were collected remain essential for drawing meaningful conclusions.
